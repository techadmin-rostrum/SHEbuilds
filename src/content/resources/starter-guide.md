Welcome! This is your SHEbuilds starter guide, helping you go from a small idea to a real business you can run from your home or neighbourhood.

The process of launching a small business consists of simple steps — you don’t need to be perfect, rich or fully ready. Just start one step at a time.

## Step 1: Discover Your Business Idea

Every good business starts with something simple. It can be something you already know how to do, enjoy doing, or see people needing around you.

You do not need to be highly educated or own a shop. You can start with your own skills, your hands, your home, or even your kitchen.

### What could your business idea be?

Think about:

- What do you enjoy doing in your daily life?
- What are you good at? (Something you do well or people appreciate)
- What skill do you already have (even a small one) that someone else might pay for?
- What is missing in your neighbourhood that women or families need?

### What can you start with?

Here are some real-life examples from women like you:

- **Stitching and Tailoring:** “I already stitch and tailor my children’s clothes. I can offer stitching services for other women in my area.”
- **Cooking:** “Everyone in my neighbourhood loves my mango pickle. I can make small jars of mango pickle and sell them.”
- **Beauty Services:** “There is no one in my area offering mehndi for brides. I can learn the basics and start at home.”
- **Childcare help:** “Many mothers work during the day. I can look after 5–6 small children in my home or set up a small day care centre.”
- **Tuition classes:** “I am good at maths. I can teach children in my area for a small price.”

> **Action Step:** Write down your ideas. Take a pen and a notebook. Write 2 or 3 ideas that come to your mind. Use the Business Idea Exploration questions on the homepage if you need prompts.

## Step 2: Check If People Around You Want It

Now that you have 2–3 simple business ideas, see which one is useful for people in your area — before you spend time or money.

You don’t need to do anything formal. Just talk to people, observe your surroundings, and listen carefully.

### Two simple ways to check

1. **Talk to 3–5 people you know and trust.** Ask neighbours, friends, or relatives something like: “If I started making [your idea], would that be helpful for you? Would you want to try it or buy it from me?”

   *Real example:* “I asked 4 women nearby if they would buy homemade rotis for lunch. Three said yes, and one said she might need it during the school season only.”

2. **Look at shops, stalls or services nearby.** Visit your local market or walk around your area. Ask:

   - Are people already selling what you want to offer?
   - If yes — can you do it differently or better?
   - If not — maybe there’s a gap you can fill!

   *Real example:* “In my area, 3 people sell pickles which are all store bought. I can offer mango pickle instead which is fresh and homemade in reusable jars.”

### What to pay attention to

| What to Look For | What to Write Down |
| --- | --- |
| What people say they need | “Many women said they want affordable blouses.” |
| What is missing in the area | “No one is selling custom-stitched kids’ clothes.” |
| What people are already buying | “Factory-made kurtas are common but poor quality.” |

> **Action Step:** In your notebook, list what you found out — who liked the idea, what is missing, and what people already buy.

Remember: your idea doesn’t need to be perfect. You are checking if it is useful, needed and welcomed in your area.

## Step 3: Know Who Will Buy From You

Understanding your target customer helps you set a fair price, make the product just right, and tell the right people about it.

### Finding the right audience

Ask yourself:

- Who would need what I am offering?
- Why would they want to buy it?
- What matters most to them — low price, homemade quality, or convenience?

Things to notice:

1. **Age & Role:** Is she a mother? A student? A bride? A worker?
2. **Money Level:** Can they pay a little more for better quality, or do they need the lowest price?
3. **Shopping Habit:** Do they buy from shops, neighbours, markets, or WhatsApp groups?
4. **What they care about:** Homemade goods? Fast service? Good packaging? Safe for kids?

| My Business Idea | My Customer |
| --- | --- |
| Making affordable blouses | Local mothers who can’t travel far for tailoring |
| Cooking lunch tiffins | Women who work in shops or schools and don’t have time to cook |
| Offering bridal mehndi | Brides in nearby villages during wedding season |
| Selling homemade soap | Families who want skin-safe, natural products |

> **Action Step:** Complete this sentence: “My customer is a ___ who wants ___ because ___.”

Your business will grow faster if you understand your customer’s life. You are not just selling a product — you are solving a real need.

## Step 4: Start Small

You do not need a shop, big money, or lots of equipment to begin. You can start from home, using skills and items you already have. Even serving 3 to 5 people you trust is a strong beginning.

1. **Choose ONE simple product or service** — something you already know how to do.
2. **Offer it to 3–5 people you trust** — neighbours, sisters-in-law, or local market sellers. You can offer a low price or free trial first.
3. **Ask for honest feedback** — what they liked or didn’t like.
4. **Make one small change** based on that feedback.

### Real examples

- **Beauty Services:** “I did threading and a basic facial for 3 neighbors. One said she would like an oil head massage too, so I’ll learn and offer that next week.”
- **Stitching:** “I stitched a blouse for my cousin. She liked the fit but asked for a deeper neckline next time.”
- **Snacks:** “I made 5 packets of masala papad and gave them to friends. They said it was tasty, but I should use less salt.”

### Simple checklist

- [ ] I chose ONE thing to start with
- [ ] I offered it to 3–5 people I know
- [ ] I asked what they liked or didn’t like
- [ ] I improved one thing based on their feedback

Remember: you don’t need to be perfect. You just need to begin. Even 1 customer is a big start.

## Step 5: Set a Simple Price

Price should cover your costs and leave a little for yourself.

1. Add up the cost of materials for one item or service.
2. Add a fair amount for your time.
3. Add a small profit.
4. Check if people nearby will pay that price — adjust if needed.

## Step 6: Tell People About Your Business

Start with people you know. Then expand:

- Share on WhatsApp with neighbours and family groups
- Tell customers who already bought from you
- Put a simple notice at the local shop or temple notice board
- Use Instagram when you are ready (even simple phone photos help)

## Step 7: Keep Learning and Growing

After a few weeks, review:

- What sold well?
- What feedback did you get?
- Can you add one new product or serve more customers?
- Can you save a little each week for better tools?

> Your business journey is a path, not a race. Take one step at a time — SHEbuilds is here to walk with you.
