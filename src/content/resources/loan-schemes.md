These are trusted government schemes that offer loans to women who want to start or grow a small business. Each one is slightly different. Read carefully to see which one fits your idea best.

You do not need to repay these all at once — they are loans with low interest, given by banks or government agencies.

*Remember: there are many more schemes that differ by state.*

## Extra notes for all schemes

- If you can’t read or write, ask a local teacher, NGO worker, or family member to help fill forms.
- Always carry original and photocopies of your documents.
- You may need to open a bank account if you don’t already have one.
- Some schemes need a guarantor or a group application (SHGs or NGOs).
- If you feel unsure, visit your local Common Service Centre (CSC) for help.

## 1. Stree Shakti Yojana

Loans up to ₹25 lakh to help women start or grow a business, often with a lower interest rate if eligible. Supported by banks like SBI and Canara Bank.

**Who can apply:**

- Woman must own most of the business (more than 51%)
- Age: 18 to 65 years
- Apply alone, with a partner, or in a Self-Help Group (SHG)
- Must attend a short Entrepreneurship Development Programme (EDP)

**How to apply:**

1. Go to a nearby bank branch (SBI, Canara Bank, etc.)
2. Bring your business idea, ID, address proof, and bank documents
3. Ask for the Stree Shakti Yojana loan application

*(You cannot apply online.)*

## 2. Stand Up India Scheme

Loans between ₹10 lakh and ₹1 crore to help women (and SC/ST individuals) start a **new** business — factory, shop, or service (tailoring, computer repair, etc.).

**Who can apply:**

- Woman (or SC/ST category)
- Brand-new business — not one already started
- 18 years or older
- No default on earlier loans

**How to apply:**

1. Online at [standupmitra.in](https://www.standupmitra.in), **or**
2. Go directly to a bank and ask for Stand Up India help

**Bring:**

- A clear business plan (what you sell/do, who customers are, how much you will earn)
- KYC: Aadhaar, PAN, passport-size photo
- Bank account details

You can also ask your local CSC or a government-recognised loan facilitation agency for help.

## 3. Annapurna Scheme

For women starting or expanding small **food** businesses — catering, tiffins, or home cooking. Loan up to ₹50,000.

**Who can apply:**

- Women in food-related work
- A simple plan (what you cook, where you sell, what items you need)

**How to apply:**

1. Visit a public sector bank (SBI, PNB, etc.)
2. Ask for the Annapurna Scheme loan
3. Carry: Aadhaar and address proof, bank passbook, priced list of tools/items, business plan

Banks may ask for security or a guarantor.

## 4. Mahila Samridhi Yojana (MSY)

Small loans for women from poor families, especially in rural and semi-urban areas, for self-employment or small business.

**Who can apply:**

- Women age 18 and above
- Economically weaker backgrounds
- Living in rural or semi-urban areas
- Plan for business like tailoring, mehendi, bangles, or pickles

**Bring:**

- Aadhaar and address proof
- Ration card (if available)
- Income certificate or self-declaration
- Business idea or plan

Run with state agencies — local rules can vary.

## 5. TREAD Scheme (Trade Related Entrepreneurship Assistance)

Gives both a **grant** (free money) and a **loan** to groups of women starting non-farm small businesses, through a government-approved NGO. Includes training and guidance.

**Who can apply:**

- Connected with an NGO (registered women’s group or organisation)
- Non-farm sector — weaving, craftwork, packaging, tailoring, etc.

**How to apply:**

1. Ask your local women’s NGO or SHG coordinator if they take part in TREAD
2. They help write the proposal, gather documents, and submit to SIDBI or the handling agency

The government gives **30%** of the business cost as a grant; the remaining **70%** is a bank loan you repay over time.
